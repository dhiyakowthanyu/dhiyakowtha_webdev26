Accessibility Design

Focus

Plain Language Rewrite

The Problem

A policy written for lawyers, not people

ADAPT Community Network supports people with developmental disabilities. Their Human Rights Committee Policy Manual is a critical document — it outlines the rights of every person ADAPT supports, what protections they have, and how decisions about their care are made.

The problem? The original document was written in dense legal and clinical language. The very people it was meant to protect often couldn't understand it.

"The people this document is for should be able to read and understand it — not just lawyers and clinicians."
📄

Original ADAPT policy document

Dense legal language, complex sentences

Process

How I approached the rewrite

01

Read & audit the original

Read through the full original policy manual and highlighted every sentence that was jargon-heavy, passive voice, or over 25 words long.

02

Research plain language principles

Applied federal plain language guidelines — active voice, short sentences, simple words, and defining terms the first time they appear.

03

Rewrite section by section

Rewrote each section maintaining all legal meaning while dramatically reducing reading level and sentence complexity.

04

Add definitions inline

Any technical term that had to stay (like "maladaptive behavior" or "informed consent") was defined immediately in plain terms.

Before & After

Plain language in action

Same meaning. Completely different reading experience.

Before — Original

"ADAPT Community Network is dedicated to respecting and honoring the human rights of all supported persons. ADAPT is committed to ensure the human rights of the persons it supports are continuously upheld and not arbitrarily diminished."

Reading level: Grade 16+ · Sentence length: 38 words

After — Plain Language

"ADAPT Community Network is dedicated to respecting and honoring the human rights of all people who need support. ADAPT is committed to making sure that we are upholding the human rights of the people we support and that they are not unfairly ignored."

Reading level: Grade 6 · Sentence length: 20 words

Before — Original

"The primary goal of the HRC is to ensure that the human rights of supported persons are appropriately protected. As part of this goal, the committee seeks to ensure that maladaptive behaviors are addressed through positive interventions and proactive techniques."

No definition of "maladaptive" · Assumes clinical knowledge

After — Plain Language

"The main goal of the Human Rights Committee (HRC), is to make sure the human rights of all supported persons are properly protected."

What is maladaptive behavior? Maladaptive behavior is a way of acting or reacting that doesn't help someone in the long run. It might make you feel better for a little while, but hurts you or others rather than fixing the issue.

Terms defined inline · Conversational tone

Before — Original

"ADAPT currently prohibits the use of any, and all techniques that include the following: corporal punishment, seclusion, noxious or aversive stimuli, forced exercise, PRN orders for psychoactive medications and denial of a nutritionally adequate diet."

Legal jargon · No plain explanation of what these mean

After — Plain Language

ADAPT will never allow certain things like:

  • Hitting people
  • Making them stay in a room alone
  • Using bad smells or pain
  • Making them do too much exercise
  • Giving medicine whenever a staff person wants
  • Not giving them enough good food

Concrete list · Everyday language · Scannable

Key Takeaways

What I learned

✍️
Plain language is a design decision

Word choice is just as important as visual design. Clarity is accessibility.

👥
Know your reader

The people this policy protects are the most important audience — not the lawyers who wrote it.

🔍
Define terms inline

Never assume the reader knows clinical or legal vocabulary. Define it the first time, every time.

♿
Accessibility goes beyond visuals

Screen readers help blind users. Plain language helps everyone — including people with cognitive disabilities.
